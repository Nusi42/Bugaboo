# The calendar

The calendar shows the issues of a search on the days they are due, as a month or as a week, so you can see what is due when and move a date by dragging an issue to another day.

## Show a search as a calendar

Open a search, a saved search or the list of a project. Above the list, set **Show as** to **Calendar**. The tools above the days set how it looks:

- **Dates from** chooses the date field the issues stand on: *Due date* at the start, or *Start date*, or an own date field of your organization. An issue stands on one day only, the day of that field. An issue that has no day in it is not on the calendar, and neither is an issue whose date you may not read.
- The steps **Earlier** and **Later** go a month or a week back and forward; **Today** comes back to today.
- **Month** and **Week** choose how much the calendar shows. A month shows the weeks that hold it, so the days around it are drawn quieter. The week starts on the day you chose in *My settings › Region*.

Everything you set is part of the view. **Save search** keeps it with the search, and the address of the page holds it too, so an address you share opens the same calendar. Which month or week is shown is not kept: a saved calendar opens around the day you open it.

A project's calendar shows the project and the projects inside it. The calendar shows at most 200 issues for the days shown; when there are more, it says so, and a shorter period or a narrower search shows the rest.

## What the calendar shows

- Each day has its number. **Today** has its number in the accent color.
- An **issue** shows its status icon, its Issue ID and its title, in the tint of its status group, like a row of the list. A press opens the issue.
- A day with more lines than fit shows **+5 more** under them. It opens the whole list of that day, with the same menu on every issue.
- A **goal** stands on its *Due date* and a **release** on its *Release date* as a mark with a diamond. They are not dragged on the calendar: the date of a release changes in its window (see [Releases](../issues/releases.md)). A calendar of every project shows the due dates of goals only; the releases are marked in the calendar of a project.

Issues you may not see are not on the calendar at all.

## Move an issue to another day

Drag the issue to another day. The date field shown changes, and the page says it with **Undo**, for example "WEB-140 due date moved to Fri 16 Oct". Only the issue you moved changes place; nothing else on the page moves.

Every drag has other ways:

- **Change due date …** (it carries the name of the field shown: *Change start date …* when the calendar stands on the start date) is in the menu of every issue, the button with three dots. It opens a box for the day, which you type in the form of your region. It is the same change as a drag, with the same **Undo**.
- With the keyboard, go to an issue and press the spacebar to pick it up. The left and right arrow keys carry it by a day, the up and down arrow keys by a week. The spacebar puts it down, and Escape puts it back where it was. A dashed place shows the day it is over until you put it down.

If somebody changed the issue after the calendar was read, nothing of theirs is overwritten. The page says who changed it and asks **Keep Ben's change** or **Use mine**, as the issue page does, and the issue stays where you put it until you answer.

An issue you may not change cannot be dragged, has no menu, and says why when you point at it: "You may not change the due date of WEB-140."

## The calendar on a phone

A phone shows a list of the days to come, six weeks from today, instead of a grid. Days without issues or marks are left out. Nothing is dragged: **Change due date …** stands beside each issue and opens the same box.
