# Who sees people

Names of people appear in many places: who an issue is assigned to, who wrote a comment, who is editing, who follows, and more. For each role and each **kind of people information** you choose how people are shown:

- **Names**: the name of the person.
- **Role only**: the role, for example "a Team member".
- **Hidden**: "Someone".

The kinds are: Assigned to, Reported by, Last changed by, Authors of comments and history, "... is editing", Followers, Time entries, Mentions, Pictures of people and Project members.

## Reading the effect

Under the table, the page shows what the chosen role sees, in three short lines, for example "Assigned to a Team member", "Comment by a Team member" and "A Team member is editing the description". Choose another role in the list above the lines to see theirs. A Guest starts without names for most kinds.

## For the whole organization and for a project

It works like [field permissions](field-permissions.md): the page in *Organization settings*, below "People", sets the role's own levels; the page of a project (*Project settings › Who sees people*) sets levels for that project and the projects inside it, says where each level comes from, and has **Reset**.

## Who always sees names

People who manage people or roles (administrators, and project leads in their projects) always see names in *Organization settings*, in *Who has which role* and in *Check access*, because they cannot do their work without them. Everybody else sees people in those places by the kind *Project members*.

## What never happens

A role label and "Someone" carry nothing that identifies the person, so a browser cannot tell two hidden people apart. E-mail addresses are never part of what a role sees; only the person and people who manage people see them.
