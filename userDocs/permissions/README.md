# What roles see, and checking access

A **role** says what a person may do. Two more settings say what each role *sees*: which fields it may see or change, and whether people are shown by name. A third page answers the question that follows: what can this person do here, and why?

- [Field permissions](field-permissions.md): for each role, which fields are hidden, can be seen or can be changed
- [Who sees people](who-sees-people.md): for each role and each kind of people information, names, role only or nobody
- [Check access](check-access.md): choose a person and a place and read what they can do, in sentences

## Where to find them

- **Organization settings**, below the heading "People": the three pages for the whole organization. They show up when you may decide what roles see (*Field permissions* and *Who sees people*) or check access.
- **Project settings** (`/p/WEB/settings`): the same three pages for one project are tabs next to *Name and place* and *Who has which role*. A project lead reaches them there, because a lead decides in their own projects only.

## The same rules everywhere

- A level you set in a project counts in the projects inside it, until one of them sets its own. Every level says where it comes from: *Set for this project*, *From Acme Corporation*, or *The role's own level*.
- You can set a level only up to your own. Levels above it are greyed out.
- A project you may not see is never named. Where a reason would name it, you read *a project you cannot see*.
- On a phone, the table of roles by fields turns into a list: choose a role, then read and change its levels one under the other.
