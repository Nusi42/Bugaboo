Bugaboo UX design

AcceptedPhase 2 of 5·2026-10-05

How Bugaboo looks, feels and behaves. Feature IDs (like ISS-04) refer to the feature list. All names follow the glossary. The clickable mockup shows the same design in action; this page is the source of truth.

How to give feedbackUse this page's comment button. Mention the section number shown next to each heading (for example §4.4), or select text and comment on it. Tapping a section number copies it, ready to paste into your comment.
Design target: people less technical than the average person. If someone who has never used a bug tracker can't figure out a screen without help, the screen is wrong. Experts get speed through shortcuts and Advanced search, but never at the cost of simplicity for everyone else.

UX principles

The numbers matter: other sections refer to them (for example "principle 6").

  1. Plain words. Every label, button and message uses everyday language from the glossary. No jargon, no abbreviations, and no names copied from other products.
  2. Words next to icons. Icons always come with a text label, except where the meaning is universal and a tooltip explains it (close ✕, search).
  3. One obvious next step. Every screen has one main action that stands out. Rare or advanced options sit behind More options.
  4. It works without setup. A new project comes with ready-made issue types, statuses, fields and roles. Nobody has to configure anything before creating the first issue.
  5. Edit where you see it. Click any value you may change to change it. There are no separate "edit pages".
  6. Show, then do. Before anything affects many items or other people, say in plain words what will happen: "12 issues will move to Done. 2 will be skipped because you can't change them."
  7. Undo instead of "Are you sure?". Reversible actions happen at once and offer Undo. Confirmations are only for things that cannot be undone.
  8. No dead ends. Empty screens say what to do next. Errors say what happened and how to fix it. Every setting has a one-line explanation and a Learn more link.
  9. Shortcuts are extras, never required. Every keyboard shortcut and gesture (swipe, long-press, drag) also has a visible button or menu item.
  10. Hidden stays hidden; read-only is explained. Things you may not see are not shown at all: no empty slots, no greyed-out placeholders, no counts. Things you may see but not change look normal, and hovering or tapping says why: "Only team members can change Priority."
  11. The assistant suggests, people decide. AI output is always labelled, and every change it proposes is shown before anything is saved.
  12. Feels instant, sends little. Changes show immediately and save in the background. Each screen asks the server only for what it shows (PERF-02).

Short names (M2-Q4): 2 to 10 capital letters or digits, starting with a letter (WEB, APP2), unique in the organization. A project lead can rename a project at any time. Changing its short name also needs the right to make projects where the project sits (Make projects for a top-level project, Make sub-projects in the parent for a sub-project), so that a short name that is taken can be told to them; the old address (/p/OLD) keeps working and the old short name stays with the project.   • Long text (description, long-text fields, a comment being edited, document pages, workflow, layout and theme editors) is locked to one person while they type. The lock starts with the first keystroke, so just reading never blocks anyone.
  • Others see who is editing and since when, or "Someone is editing" if their role may not see names (§5.2). They can Ask Ana to finish (sends her a notification) or, if allowed, Edit anyway: Ana is told, and her unsaved text is kept as her draft.
  • The lock keeps itself alive while the editor is open. If the tab closes or the connection drops, it frees itself within a minute.
  • Nothing is lost: while typing, drafts are kept in the browser and privately on the server.
  • Every change is confirmed: it shows at once as Saving…, then Saved. If the server says no (someone changed it, the lock ran out, offline), the screen says what happened in plain words, keeps the text, and offers Compare and save.
  • Short values (status, priority, people, dates) don't lock. They save instantly and are checked against the latest version.
  • Until live updates arrive, open issues are checked every 30 seconds and when you come back to the tab: "Ben changed the description after you."

Related issues

  • Grouped by what the relation means, read from this issue's side: blocks, is blocked by, duplicate of, has duplicate, causes, is caused by, related to. Every relation shows on both issues, each in its own direction.
  • Relations can cross projects. You only see a relation when you may see both issues.
  • Sub-issues stay a separate block, because they describe a parent and its parts, not a relation between equals.
  • Two buttons under the block: Add relation and Show relation map (§4.15).

Working hours

  • Start work day, Start break, End break and End work day are one tap away: in the sidebar on computers, at the top of Home and in the bottom bar's timer on phones.
  • Forgot to tap? Type start, end and breaks into the timesheet's Work day rows afterwards.
  • Working-time rules (Germany preset, editable by admins): at most 10 hours a day, 30 min break after 6 hours and 45 min after 9, 11 hours of rest between two days. A broken rule shows a plain warning on that day. It never blocks recording, because the record must show what really happened.
  • Changing a past day asks for a reason. The old value stays in the history, and if someone else changed it, the person gets a notification.
  • Who sees it: everyone sees their own working hours. Seeing other people's needs the separate permission See working hours of others. Team reports can be set to show totals only. Working hours are never used for scores or rankings.
  • Kept for 2 years in the Germany preset, then deleted automatically (§5.7).

Time on issues

  • The timer is always visible: at the bottom of the sidebar on computers, at the top of Home on phones. Tap it for Stop · Switch issue · Change start time · Throw away.
  • Stopping the timer asks for an optional note and shows Activity and Cost center, already filled in from the issue (or its project).
  • Activity (Development, Testing, Documentation, Meetings, Support) and Cost center (number and name) are ordinary fields: admins can rename them, change the lists, make them required, or add more fields to time entries.
  • Timesheet: one row per issue, one column per day. Type 1:30, 1.5 or 90m; all mean ninety minutes. The footer compares working hours with time booked on issues, so gaps are easy to spot.
  • Reports: use the same search bar. Group by person, project, issue, activity, cost center, any own field, or week. Shown as table and bar chart, with Download as spreadsheet (CSV).
  • Closed months (1.0): once a month is closed, changes need a reason.

First start and signing in

Setup in the browser (after installing)

  1. Welcome: type the setup code shown by the server (AUTH-09). The page explains where to find it.
  2. Your administrator account: a passkey is suggested first; a password plus two-step sign-in also works.
  3. Name and address: organization name and web address. The page explains the connection security (HTTPS) in plain words.

Relation map ISS-16

A picture of everything connected to an issue: in any direction, through any number of steps. It answers questions like "what is all this waiting on?" and "what else breaks if this slips?".

Try it: point at (or tap) WEB-148 to see how it connects back to WEB-142. The Follow chips switch kinds of relation on and off, and Show as list switches to sentences.

  • Opening it: Show relation map on any issue (§4.4), or Show as → Map for any search, which draws all results and the relations between them.
  • Steps away: 1, 2, 3 or Everything. The starting issue sits in the middle with a highlighted border.
  • Follow: switch each kind of relation on or off. Each kind has its own line: blocks (orange, arrow), causes (red, arrow), duplicate (dashed grey), related (grey, no arrow, because it has no direction), part of (violet, towards the parent). Colors are never the only signal: every line also has its words.
  • Point at an issue to highlight the chain that connects it back to the start; everything else fades. Click an issue to move it to the middle, so you can walk along a chain. Its details open in the side panel.
  • Circles in blocks chains (A blocks B blocks A) are flagged with a warning, because nothing in a circle can start.
  • List form: the same chains as sentences, grouped by steps away. This is the default on phones and what screen readers get.
  • Security: only issues you may see appear. Chains never pass through hidden issues, so a map can't reveal that a hidden issue exists, not even as a gap.
  • Speed and size: the server walks the relations, with a step limit and at most 200 issues per map. It sends only number, title, status and the relations. If there are more, the map says so and suggests fewer steps or fewer kinds.

  • Under the hood: every stored field that holds personal data is marked once in the data model. Export, removal, deletion rules and the record of processing all come from those marks, so a new feature can't forget them.

Visual design

Design tokens THM, SSOT

Code uses only named tokens, never raw colors or sizes; the hooks enforce this. The tokens live in one file (docs/design/theme.css, generated from design/theme.kdl), and this page reads its colors from that same file. Tokens have three levels:

  1. Base palettes: grey 0–1000, highlight color, red, orange, yellow, green, teal, blue, violet, pink; fonts, sizes, corner radii, shadows.
  2. Meaning tokens (what the theme editor mostly changes):
    • backgrounds: bg.canvas, bg.surface, bg.raised, bg.sunken, bg.hover, bg.selected
    • text: fg.default, fg.muted, fg.subtle, fg.onAccent
    • lines: border.default, border.subtle, focus.ring
    • highlight: accent.solid, accent.soft, accent.fg
    • messages: success.*, warning.*, danger.*, info.*
    • status groups: status.notStarted.*, status.inProgress.*, status.done.*
    • tag colors: cat.1 … cat.12, each a background/text pair, used for labels, tags, choices and charts
    • relation lines: relation.blocks, relation.causes, relation.duplicate, relation.related, relation.partOf
  3. Component tokens (only where needed), for example button.primary.bg.

Each tag color is checked for readability in every built-in theme. A color picked once reads well in all four themes, which is why choices store a token (cat.4), not a color value.
