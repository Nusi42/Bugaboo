Bugaboo UX design

AcceptedPhase 2 of 5·2026-10-05, the look updated 2026-10-16

How Bugaboo looks, feels and behaves. Feature IDs (like ISS-04) refer to the feature list. All names follow the glossary. The clickable mockup shows an earlier state of the design; this page is the source of truth.

How to give feedbackUse this page's comment button. Mention the section number shown next to each heading (for example §4.4), or select text and comment on it. Tapping a section number copies it, ready to paste into your comment.
Design target: people less technical than the average person. If someone who has never used a bug tracker can't figure out a screen without help, the screen is wrong. Experts get speed through shortcuts and Advanced search, but never at the cost of simplicity for everyone else.

UX principles

The numbers matter: other sections refer to them (for example "principle 6").

  1. Plain words. Every label, button and message uses everyday language from the glossary. No jargon, no abbreviations, and no names copied from other products.
  2. Words next to icons. Icons always come with a text label, except where the meaning is universal and a tooltip explains it (close ✕, search).
  3. One obvious next step. Every screen has one main action that stands out. Rare or advanced options sit behind More options.
  4. It works without setup. A new project comes with ready-made issue types, statuses, fields and roles. Nobody has to configure anything before creating the first issue.
  5. Edit where you see it. Click any value you may change to change it. There are no separate "edit pages".
  6. Show, then do. Before anything affects many items or other people, say in plain words what will happen: "12 issues will move to Done. 2 will be skipped because you can't change them."
  7. Undo instead of "Are you sure?". Reversible actions happen at once and offer Undo. Confirmations are only for things that cannot be undone.
  8. No dead ends. Empty screens say what to do next. Errors say what happened and how to fix it. Every setting has a one-line explanation and a Learn more link.
  9. Shortcuts are extras, never required. Every keyboard shortcut and gesture (swipe, long-press, drag) also has a visible button or menu item.
  10. Hidden stays hidden; read-only is explained. Things you may not see are not shown at all: no empty slots, no greyed-out placeholders, no counts. Things you may see but not change look normal, and hovering or tapping says why: "Only team members can change Priority."
  11. The assistant suggests, people decide. AI output is always labelled, and every change it proposes is shown before anything is saved.
  12. Feels instant, sends little. Changes show immediately and save in the background. Each screen asks the server only for what it shows (PERF-02).

Ten rules for the look

Every screen follows these rules. Reviews check them on every screenshot: alignment, padding and state colors (definition of done). Other sections refer to them as R1 … R10.

  1. R1 · One left edge. Text, lists, forms and buttons line up on the left edge of their container. Only an empty state is centered.
  2. R2 · One spacing scale. 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 (§7.4). Inside a control 12, inside a box 16, inside a dialog or a page 24, between sections 24 to 32.
  3. R3 · No text touches a line. Every box with a border or a fill has inner padding of at least 12. Lists inside a box keep 16 on the left.
  4. R4 · Color means state. Color is kept for the status group, the priority, notes and the one main button. A row is tinted by its status group (all three groups, Not started too) and edged by its priority. Issue types and people stay neutral; a label shows its own color as a small square.
  5. R5 · Never color alone. A status always has its icon and its word. A priority always has its icon, and its word where there is room. Colors also differ in lightness.
  6. R6 · One main action per area. One filled button per screen part, at the right end of its header or footer. Others are outlined or quiet. A button says what it does: "Change 12 issues", not "Apply".
  7. R7 · Change in place. Short values save as soon as they change and say Saved, with Undo. Descriptions and comments keep Edit and Save, because two people may want them at once (edit lock, §4.4).
  8. R8 · Two text colors, few links. Normal and muted. Accent-colored links only lead somewhere. A whole row is clickable and is not underlined.
  9. R9 · Same thing, same place. Status, ID, title, priority, assigned person: always in this order, in the list, the issue page, sub-issues, related issues and on the phone.
  10. R10 · Forms open as windows. A question is a small, fixed dialog (440 wide). A form is a window: minimize, maximize and close at the top right, resize at any edge, maximize fills the page; title and buttons always visible (§6.3).

  • One grid with fixed columns (R1, R9): the project's default columns are tick box, status icon, ID (mono), title (normal text, not bold, not a link color), priority, assigned person and changed. Type and labels are not in the default list, so the title keeps its room; Customize adds them, and any other field you may read. The title keeps its least width of 160 px. Where the list is narrower than its columns (beside an open issue, with Customize open) the columns of fields (assigned to, changed, project, labels, an own field) are dropped from the right, one by one, until the rest fits, counting the widths you dragged; the status icon, the ID, the title and the priority stay, and the others come back when there is room. Beside an open issue the list has the room that is left, 16 px from the panel. Only where even those do not fit the list scrolls sideways, and its head scrolls with the rows. A word that does not fit ends in an ellipsis, in a cell and in the head. Everything is left-aligned; the status icon, the ID and the priority sit in fixed columns, so the eye can run down them. A whole row is clickable (R8).
  • Rows carry their state (R4): the row is clearly tinted by its status group (Not started grey, In progress blue, Done green; see §7.2) and edged on the left by its priority (4 px, Critical red, High a clear orange of its own that is told from the red by a difference of color, none for Normal and Low; under forced colors Critical is a double bar and High a single bar). A done issue's title turns muted. The tint is the strongest one that still keeps every text color at 4.5:1 (7:1 with more contrast, and the quiet words of a row at 5:1), and the three tints differ clearly from each other and from the card; a row keeps its tint when it is hovered, selected or current: the state shows by a thin 1 px line on hover, the ticked box and an accent line under the row when ticked (the ring is for the issue that is open), and a double ring (accent and text color) for the keyboard focus, never by changing the fill.
  • Priority in the list: only Critical shows its word; the others show their icon, with the word as its accessible name (R5).
  • Groups: Group by status is the default, and the groups come In progress, Not started, Done; priorities come highest first (the order of a list; sorting uses another, see architecture 04 search). Each group header shows the group's icon and color and the number of issues. Group by works for any field you can see. A folded group is left out of the rows shown and ticked.
  • Legend: a small legend beside the toolbar says what the row color and the left edge mean. Where it has no room (on a phone, and beside an open issue) it opens from a Legend button in a sheet from the bottom.
  • Above the list: the title Issues, how many there are and how many are shown ("302 in Website · 13 shown"), the filter chips, Group by and Spacing: Comfortable (rows 44 with a mouse, 64 with touch) or Compact.
  • Columns are chosen with Customize → Columns (a checklist, moved with Up and Down; drag to reorder comes later). Resize columns by dragging their edges, or with the arrow keys on the edge, and sort by clicking the column name.
  • Change in place: click a status, person, priority or date to change it right there; it says Saved with Undo (R7).
  • Ticked issues: a bar under the list, stuck to the bottom of the window on a computer (it moves nothing above it), says "2 issues are ticked" and offers Change 2 issues, Move to a project and Delete 2 issues (Delete is the red button). A ticked row keeps its tint and shows the ticked box and an accent line under it; only the open issue has a ring. The box in the head shows a dash while some of the rows shown are ticked. Before applying, it says what will happen and which issues will be skipped (principle 6).
  • With the keyboard: the list is one stop for Tab; the arrow keys (and j and k) move over the group headers and the rows, Enter on a header folds its group and Enter on a row opens its issue. The head has column headers that say the order they sort in, and the edge of a column is a separator whose width the arrow keys change.
  • The list loads more while you scroll. Very long lists stay fast because only the visible rows are drawn.
  • Tick all results (M5): the tick box in the head of the list ticks the rows shown; when more issues match, the bar offers "Tick all 302 results". Up to 5,000 issues can be ticked this way; above that the bar says "Narrow the search to 5,000 issues or fewer to change them together." (with an info icon, in the same bar).
  • Customize → Columns (M5): a panel with every field you may see as a checklist; it stands beside the list where the window has room for both (from about 1280 wide) and under it where it has not. Ticked columns are on top in their order, moved with Up and Down (drag to reorder is not part of this version). Back to the project's columns undoes your changes. The ID and the title cannot be taken away.
  • The Search page (M5): Search in the sidebar opens the same list over every project you can see, with a Project column. A project's list is this page with Project: Website filled in; removing that filter widens it to all projects.
  • Empty and error states (M5): no issue matches → "No issues match this search." with Remove all filters; the search took too long → "This search takes too long. Add a filter, for example a project or a status."; a mistake in Advanced search → the list keeps the last results at full contrast, under a blue note (§6.1) that says they are the last ones, until the search is fixed (no dashed edge: it would look like a place to drop something); when there is no earlier list (the address itself has the mistake) there is no head and no such note, only the mistake.
  • A changed saved search says Changed, not saved and offers Undo changes next to Save.
  • On a phone (M5): the list shows one card per issue (status icon, ID, title, priority, assigned person); columns are not offered. The page shows its title, New issue and Filters; Group by and Spacing are in the Filters sheet and the colour legend opens from a small Legend button (owner decision, 2026-10-29). Opening an issue opens its full page (§3.4).

  • Files (ATT-01, ADR-0032): a block with a row per file: its preview (images and the first page of a PDF) or its type's icon, the name, the size, who added it and when, and Download. To add files, drag them onto the issue, paste a screenshot, or use Upload. Each upload shows its progress where the file will be and goes on by itself after a lost connection. A file that is too big or of a type the organization does not accept is refused at once with the limit in words: "Too big: at most 25 MB per file.". Files you added, or any file if you may change the issue, have Delete, which goes to the trash with Undo. Clicking a preview shows it larger. Other files are only downloaded, never opened inside the page. Comments show their files in the same rows under the text.

Agents AI-30…38, ADR-0029

An agent is an account of its own that works on issues a person hands over. It has one responsible person and can never do more than that person. Bugaboo does not run agents; they connect from outside through MCP (§4.12).

  • Hand over (a window, §6.3): from one or several issues, pick the agent, check the brief (description, steps, Done when, a note; a missing Done when is asked for), tick what it may do, set a budget and whether its Plan needs approval (it then waits for Approve plan). Visual design

Design tokens THM, SSOT

Code uses only named tokens, never raw colors or sizes; the hooks enforce this. The tokens live in one file (docs/design/theme.css, generated from design/theme.kdl), and this page reads its colors from that same file. Tokens have three levels:

  1. Base palettes: grey 0–1000, highlight color, red, orange, yellow, green, teal, blue, violet, pink; fonts, sizes, corner radii, shadows.
  2. Meaning tokens (what the theme editor mostly changes):
    • backgrounds: bg.canvas, bg.surface, bg.raised, bg.sunken, bg.hover, bg.selected
    • text: fg.default, fg.muted, fg.subtle, fg.onAccent
    • lines: border.default, border.subtle, focus.ring
    • highlight: accent.solid, accent.soft, accent.fg
    • messages: success.*, warning.*, danger.*, info.*
    • status groups: status.notStarted.*, status.inProgress.*, status.done.*
    • tag colors: cat.1 … cat.12, each a background/text pair, used for labels, tags, choices and charts
    • relation lines: relation.blocks, relation.causes, relation.duplicate, relation.related, relation.partOf
  3. Component tokens (only where needed), for example button.primary.bg.

Each tag color is checked for readability in every built-in theme. A color picked once reads well in all four themes, which is why choices store a token (cat.4), not a color value.
