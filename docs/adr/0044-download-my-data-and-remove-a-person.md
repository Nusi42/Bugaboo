# ADR-0044: Download my data and Remove a person

Status: **Accepted** · 2026-11-18 · refines PRIV-03, PRIV-04, PRIV-06 and [ADR-0015](0015-personal-data-marks.md), [ADR-0022](0022-who-sees-people.md), [ADR-0035](0035-time-on-issues-and-working-hours.md) decision 7; the answers to M9-Q3 to M9-Q8 and M9-Q11 of [the M9 plan](../milestones/M9/README.md)

## Context

The GDPR gives every person the right to a copy of what an organization stores about them, with the information of Art. 15(1) (purposes, legal basis, recipients, how long it is kept), the right to take it elsewhere (Art. 20) and, within limits, the right to have it erased (Art. 17). Bugaboo promised both from the start: every stored field carries a personal-data mark (ADR-0015), and the marks were meant to drive *Download my data* and the *Remove a person* plan. Neither exists yet. Eight milestones of follow-ups wait for them: the events about a person's settings (M2-F75), locks, drafts and notes (M3-F34), file bytes (M4-F52), notifications (M4-F64), the full-text index (M5-F52), private and shared saved searches (M5-F62, M5-F63), agents (M7-F36), the mark of text written with the assistant in exports (M7-F65), work times (M8-F30) and the choice of a theme (M8-F74).

The marks say *whether* a field is personal and on which legal basis. They do not say *whose* data a row is. A comment has an author; a notification has a recipient; an away time has its person; an event has an actor and, for a change of a setting, the person whose setting it was. Export and removal both need that answer, and they must not each write it.

The tombstone exists in part already: `bb-policy` (`people.rs`) has `PersonRef::Former`, `Target.removed` with `FormerUser.label`, and `former_user_label`, which draws the random code (tested by `priv_13_former_user_labels_are_random_and_unique`); `resolve` already keeps the name of a removed person for the work days (ADR-0035, decision 7). Nothing removes a person yet.

Some records must outlive the person's account: work days, for the keep period, with the person's name for those who may see working hours (ADR-0035, decision 7), and the security log, whose chain cannot be changed. People also leave an organization long before anybody wants their data erased: their account has to stop working at once, while the records stay.

## Decision

1. **One walk of a person's data, written once.** Every entity that holds a reference to a person says, next to its marks, which of its columns name a person and what that person is to the row (`author`, `recipient`, `subject`, `actor`, `holder`). `bb-core` holds the list (`personal::PersonLinks`, generated from the entity definitions like the marks), and `bb-store` answers it with one walk (`PersonData` port): for one person, every row of every entity that names them, by kind of link. *Download my data*, *Remove a person* and the count shown before a removal read this walk and nothing else. A new entity with a person column and no link fails the build. Enforced by: DATA-MARKS (an entity with a person column needs a link), review (the walk is the one place a person's rows are found; a second search for them in the export or the removal is a finding).
2. ***Download my data* is a file of two halves.** A person asks for it in *My settings › My data*; a job (`privacy.export`) writes one ZIP file into `exports/` in the data folder, and the person gets a notification with a signed link (5 minutes, ADR-0032) while the file is there. The file holds readable pages (plain HTML with the theme's colours in one style sheet of the file, no script, text rendered by `bb-markdown`) and the same data as JSON, one file per kind of record, with a `README` that names each field in plain words and gives, for each kind, the information of Art. 15(1): the purpose, the legal basis, how long it is kept and who receives it. Each kind of record and each of its columns is named in the message catalog (`personal-kind.<kind>`, `personal-field.<kind>.<field>`), so the headers of the pages and the lines of the `README` read in plain words and not as names from the code; `#[derive(Entity)]` takes both keys for every entity that names a person, so a kind or a field without its message does not compile. That information is generated from the marks, the keep list (ADR-0045) and the outside services that are switched on (mail server, AI provider), never written by hand. Text written with the assistant keeps its mark in both halves (COMP-09, M7-F65). The file is kept 7 days, then deleted by the keep rule *Data exports* (ADR-0045). Enforced by: SEC-MATRIX (the routes of exports for every starting role), DATA-LOGS (the job logs IDs only), DATA-MARKS (every mark carries what the README needs), review (the JSON names follow the glossary).
3. **What is in it** (M9-Q7): every row the walk finds as the person's own (their profile, settings, sessions and sign-in history, passkeys without their keys, access keys without their secrets, the issues they reported, the comments and texts they wrote with every version, their time entries, work days with breaks and the reasons of changes, work times and away times with their notes, notifications, follows, mentions of them, saved searches, agents and agent work they are responsible for, the security-log entries that name them, and the files they uploaded, bytes included). Other people appear only as the person may see them at that moment (`PersonRef`, ADR-0022): the export never shows more than the screens would. Texts of others are not copied, only named where the person is mentioned. The full-text index is left out: it is a copy of texts already in the export (M5-F52). Enforced by: ARCH-PERSON-REF, SEC-MATRIX, review (a reviewer compares the walk's kinds with the list).
4. **An administrator can make the same file for somebody else** (M9-Q8): with the new permission *Download someone's data* (organization, *Manage*; *Administrator* at the start; no access key or agent holds it), and always with a reason. It holds what the person's own export holds, whatever else the administrator may read, because it answers that person's request. The person is told by e-mail at the address on record (also when they are switched off and cannot read a notification) and in the app; the security log records who made it for whom and why. Enforced by: ARCH-PERMS, SEC-MATRIX, ARCH-EVENTS.
5. **Switch off before Remove** (M9-Q4). *Switch off* (with *Manage people*) ends a person's sign-in at once: sessions, access keys, OAuth grants of AI apps and the person's agents stop (M7-F36), invitations they made are taken back. Their standing rules end; their open assignments and project memberships stay, with the person marked *switched off* in pickers, until somebody changes them, because who takes the work over is a person's decision (M9-Q5; COMP-10). *Switch on* gives the sign-in back. A switched-off person counts as nobody for notifications, picks and limits, and keeps every record. Each switch-off is written to the replay list (decision 8). Enforced by: SEC-MATRIX (a switched-off person's session, key and agent are refused), ARCH-PERMS.
6. ***Remove a person* is final and has one plan.** With the new permission *Remove people* (organization, *Manage*; *Administrator* at the start; no key or agent), after a window that says what is deleted now, what stays with *Former user K7Q2* and what is kept by law until when, all read from the walk and the keep rules (ADR-0045). Only a switched-off person is removed. One change then:
   - deletes the account and everything that only serves it: password, passkeys, two-step data, sessions, access keys, grants, handle, settings and the choice of theme (M8-F74), private saved searches (M5-F62), drafts, locks and notes (M3-F34), notifications and follows (M4-F64), work times through `delete_work_times_in` (M8-F30), agents and standing rules (M7-F36, M9-Q5); their project memberships end, while issues assigned to them stay assigned, shown as *Former user*, for somebody to change. One table in `bb-core` (`removal.rs`) says, for each kind of record, what the person is to a row that goes, and every kind it does not name is kept (amended 2026-11-19 with what the code deletes):
     - a row goes where the person **holds** it or **is its subject** (their own sign-in, keys, grants, handle, settings, follows, the rows that give them roles and the members of groups they are in), where they **wrote** it as its only author (private saved searches, drafts, standing rules) or where they **are its recipient** (notifications, notes of an edit). A notification or a note of an edit the person caused for somebody else stays with that person, shown as *Former user*;
     - **invitations**, used or open, that the person made or got: sign-in records of the account, which tell nothing to the organization once the account is gone;
     - **files of *Download my data*** made of the person: the plan names them in words of their own, saying that a file not fetched yet goes too, so an administrator gives it to the person first;
     - **hand-overs** the person made: each gives work to one of the person's own agents, which go too; the agent work they started stays as a record of the issue, shown as *Former user*;
     - **away times**: they belong to work times and go with them through `delete_work_times_in`. Their keep period of one year (ADR-0045) is a longest time, not a time the law asks for, so it keeps nothing past the removal; work days, which the law keeps, are a kind of their own and stay;
   - makes the person a tombstone with the code that `bb-policy`'s `former_user_label` draws (no second way to draw it), stored as the `FormerUser` label that `Target.removed` carries, so every reference shows *Former user …* through `PersonRef::Former`;
   - clears the name and the e-mail address from the person's record. When the person has work days inside their keep period, the name moves into one **legal-hold field** of the tombstone, with its reason ("work days, ArbZG §16") and its end (the date of the last kept work day plus the keep period). `resolve` reads that field, and only for the working-hours context it already serves. The field is cleared in the change in which an administrator's *Delete them* (ADR-0045) deletes the person's last work days; no job clears it. Time entries do not keep the name: they show *Former user* like everything else (M9-Q6);
   - scrubs the copies of personal values in the events: the before and after of the person's own settings (M2-F75), the name and text of their private searches (M5-F63), and the values that the deleted rows carried; the changes the person made to records only their owner makes (`whose::MADE_ONLY_BY_THEIR_OWNER`: access keys, passkeys, connected AI apps, sessions), even when the record is gone; and every value that holds their full name as one phrase of whole words (a name of two words or more; a name of one word, such as "Mark", is a common word too often and is found through the person's mentions and address), one of their handles as a mention (`@` and the whole handle, the only way a handle stands in a text), or their address anywhere, capitals not counting and in any script. The database picks the rows that may hold a name by the letters of ASCII it folds like Rust; a name with none of them (Cyrillic, Greek, Chinese) makes the scrub read the events, the history and the notifications in full, by key in pages, while it holds the write lock of SQLite (measured at the exit of M9, M9-F51). What the person did to the records of others (the roles they gave, the work times they edited) keeps its values;
   - leaves the work days, their breaks and reasons, and the security log, whose entries name the person by ID and so read *Former user …* from then on.

   What cannot be done inside one change (the full-text index of the texts that change, with SQLite's `secure_delete` on the segments, M5-F52) is a job, `privacy.finish-removal`, queued in that change and safe to run again. On PostgreSQL only the reindex runs: no step a job can take makes `VACUUM` overwrite the old rows, so the database's own vacuum takes the space back in its time. Enforced by: ARCH-PERMS, SEC-MATRIX, ARCH-PERSON-REF, ARCH-EVENTS, ARCH-DB-PARITY (the removal tests on both databases).
7. **Texts the person wrote** (M9-Q3): the window offers *Keep their comments, shown as Former user* (the start, an organization setting `privacy.removed-texts`) or *Delete their comments*. Issue descriptions, fields, time entries and files they made are business records of the organization and stay, shown as *Former user*; deleting them is the ordinary deletion of each. Enforced by: SEC-MATRIX, review (no other text is deleted by the choice).
8. **The replay list: what a restore must not undo** (M9-Q11). Every removal (with its choice about comments), every switch-off, and every taking back of an access key, an AI app grant, a passkey or a support access writes one line to `privacy/replay.list` in the data folder: the kind, the ID, the moment, and for a removal the comments choice; no name and no other value. After a restore from an older backup, the restore runs every line newer than the backup again before the server opens (ADR-0046). A replayed removal does not ask for the switch-off first, and it applies the comments choice of its line, so deleted comments do not come back. *Switch on* writes no line: a restore that runs an earlier switch-off again leaves the person switched off, which fails safe, and an administrator switches them on again. A line names no administrator, so the standing of a line run again names nobody as who did it (`by` is empty: a restore did it; migration 60 lets the column be empty and empties any row that names the person themselves), and a replayed removal draws a new *Former user* label, because the line holds none; references show the new label from then on. A replay does not ask whether somebody still decides roles; when the lines it ran leave nobody who does, the restore says so in its report and in the security log, and setup is open again. A line is written in the change it stands for, into the database, and moves into the file right after the commit; a line the file does not take waits there, goes with the next write and before every backup, and the security log says that it waits. The list is kept in three places that a restore merges: the data folder, a copy inside every backup, and the copy that `bugaboo keys export` writes beside the keys, so a computer lost with its data folder does not lose the list. Enforced by: review (the restore tests of ADR-0046 cover each kind of line).
9. **Corrections** (PRIV-06): a person corrects their own name; a change of their e-mail address takes effect when they open a link sent to the new address, and the old address is told. An administrator with *Manage people* corrects the name and e-mail address of others with a reason; the person is told, and the security log records it. Other personal data already has its own way to be corrected (work days with a reason, ADR-0035; work times, ADR-0030). Enforced by: SEC-MATRIX, ARCH-EVENTS.

## Consequences

- A new entity that names a person has to declare its links, or the build fails, so export and removal cannot forget it. Enforced by: DATA-MARKS.
- The tombstone keeps every reference valid: history, comments and issues still read, with *Former user …* where a name was.
- The only place a removed person's name survives is the legal-hold field, with a reason and an end date that the removal window shows.
- Removal is not undone. The window says so, and the plan is shown before anything happens.
- Backups older than a removal still hold the person until their keep period ends; the keep rule *Backups* (ADR-0045) bounds that time, and the replay list stops a restore from bringing the person back.

## Alternatives considered

- **Each feature writes its own export and removal code:** the exact failure ADR-0015 was written to avoid. Rejected.
- **Overwrite the person's ID in every row:** breaks the security log's chain and every foreign key, and gains nothing over a tombstone whose name and e-mail are gone. Rejected.
- **Keep the whole person row while work days are held:** keeps the e-mail address and the rest for years for the one field the law needs. Rejected in favour of one legal-hold field.
- **Delete everything the person wrote:** destroys the organization's records (issues, decisions, time booked for customers) that it may keep under Art. 17(3). Rejected; the choice covers comments only.
- **Export synchronously in the request:** a person with years of work produces a large file; a job keeps the server responsive. Rejected.

## Enforced by

DATA-MARKS, ARCH-PERMS, SEC-MATRIX, ARCH-PERSON-REF, ARCH-EVENTS, ARCH-DB-PARITY, DATA-LOGS; review (the one walk, the legal-hold field, and what a replay restores).

## Amendment (M9, how corrections are kept)

- **One keyed digest.** The secret of the link to a new address, the memory of sign-up e-mails (M4-F122) and the values a correction replaces are kept as an HMAC-SHA256 under a key of the server (`bb_auth::DigestKey`). It is the same key as `keys/csrf.key`, kept apart by a name: every digest starts with a name of its own, as every signed link does, so there is no further key file to back up or rotate: what depends on it lives an hour or is only ever compared. Enforced by: review (one function makes every keyed digest).
- **What the history keeps.** A person's own new name is kept in the history in clear, as the change of a short value. A change of an address keeps only the keyed digests of both addresses, and a correction by an administrator keeps the keyed digests of the old and the new value and the reason. The security log names who changed what of whom and holds no value. Enforced by: review (the tests `priv_06_*` check the events).
- **The second check of a new address** asks for the password again; a passkey as the second check comes later (M9-F80). The link to a new address is made like the link of an invitation (`OneUseLink`, one hour, one use), and the daily job `sessions.purge` deletes the links that ran out (the row *Links that confirm a new e-mail address* of ADR-0045).

## Amendment (security fixes before the first release): the password guards an export and a removal

- ***Download someone's data* and *Remove a person* ask for the password again**, after the permission is checked: a session alone, which a thief may hold, could otherwise take the data of everybody or remove people for good. The bodies of `POST /api/v1/people/{person}/data-export` and `POST /api/v1/people/{person}/remove` gain the optional `password`; without it, or with a wrong one, the request is refused with `auth.wrong-credentials`, and a person who has no password (a passkey, company sign-in) with `auth.password-needed` until a passkey can confirm instead (M9-F200). The shape of `/api/v1` stays as it was frozen (ADR-0008: a new optional member loosens a request), but its behaviour changes: a client of the API that exports someone's data or removes a person has to send the password from now on. *Download my data* asks for nothing more: it hands a person only what is theirs. Enforced by: the tests `sec_12_a_stolen_session_cannot_download_someones_data_without_the_password` and `sec_12_a_stolen_session_cannot_remove_a_person_without_the_password`.
