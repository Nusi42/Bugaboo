# ADR-0044: Download my data and Remove a person

Status: **Proposed** · 2026-11-18 · refines PRIV-03, PRIV-04, PRIV-06 and [ADR-0015](0015-personal-data-marks.md), [ADR-0022](0022-who-sees-people.md), [ADR-0035](0035-time-on-issues-and-working-hours.md) decision 7; open questions M9-Q3 to M9-Q6 of [the M9 plan](../milestones/M9/README.md)

## Context

The GDPR gives every person the right to a copy of what an organization stores about them (Art. 15, Art. 20) and, within limits, the right to have it erased (Art. 17). Bugaboo promised both from the start: every stored field carries a personal-data mark (ADR-0015), and the marks were meant to drive *Download my data* and the *Remove a person* plan. Neither exists yet. Eight milestones of follow-ups wait for them: the events about a person's settings (M2-F75), locks, drafts and notes (M3-F34), file bytes (M4-F52), notifications (M4-F64), the full-text index (M5-F52), private and shared saved searches (M5-F62, M5-F63), agents (M7-F36), the mark of text written with the assistant in exports (M7-F65), work times (M8-F30) and the choice of a theme (M8-F74).

The marks say *whether* a field is personal and on which legal basis. They do not say *whose* data a row is. A comment has an author; a notification has a recipient; an away time has its person; an event has an actor and, for a change of a setting, the person whose setting it was. Export and removal both need that answer, and they must not each write it.

Some records must outlive the person's account: work days, for the keep period, with the person's name for those who may see working hours (ADR-0035, decision 7), and the security log, whose chain cannot be changed. People also leave an organization long before anybody wants their data erased: their account has to stop working at once, while the records stay.

## Decision

1. **One walk of a person's data, written once.** Every entity that holds a reference to a person says, next to its marks, which of its columns name a person and what that person is to the row (`author`, `recipient`, `subject`, `actor`, `holder`). `bb-core` holds the list (`personal::PersonLinks`, generated from the entity definitions like the marks), and `bb-store` answers it with one walk (`PersonData` port): for one person, every row of every entity that names them, by kind of link. *Download my data*, *Remove a person* and the count shown before a removal read this walk and nothing else. A new entity with a person column and no link fails the build. Enforced by: DATA-MARKS (an entity with a person column needs a link), review (the walk is the one place a person's rows are found; a second search for them in the export or the removal is a finding).
2. ***Download my data* is a file of two halves.** A person asks for it in *My settings › My data*; a job (`privacy.export`) writes one ZIP file into `exports/` in the data folder, and the person gets a notification with a signed link (5 minutes, ADR-0032) while the file is there. The file holds readable pages (plain HTML with the theme's colours inlined into one style sheet of the file, no script, text rendered by `bb-markdown`) and the same data as JSON, one file per kind of record, with a `README` that names each field in the words of the glossary. Text written with the assistant keeps its mark in both halves (COMP-09, M7-F65). The file is kept 7 days, then deleted by the keep rule *Data exports* (ADR-0045). Enforced by: SEC-MATRIX (the routes of exports for every starting role), DATA-LOGS (the job logs IDs only), review (the JSON names follow the glossary).
3. **What is in it** (M9-Q5, recommendation): every row the walk finds as the person's own (their profile, settings, sessions and sign-in history, passkeys without their keys, access keys without their secrets, the issues they reported, the comments and texts they wrote with every version, their time entries, work days with breaks and the reasons of changes, work times and away times with their notes, notifications, follows, mentions of them, saved searches, agents and agent work they are responsible for, the security-log entries that name them, and the files they uploaded, bytes included). Other people appear only as the person may see them at that moment (`PersonRef`, ADR-0022): the export never shows more than the screens would. Texts of others are not copied, only named where the person is mentioned. The full-text index is left out: it is a copy of texts already in the export (M5-F52). Enforced by: ARCH-PERSON-REF, SEC-MATRIX, review (a reviewer compares the walk's kinds with the list).
4. **An administrator can make the same file for somebody else** (M9-Q6, recommendation): with the new permission *Download someone's data* (organization, *Manage*; *Administrator* at the start; no access key or agent holds it). It holds what the person's own export holds, whatever else the administrator may read, because it answers that person's request; the person is told, and the security log records who made it for whom. Enforced by: ARCH-PERMS, SEC-MATRIX, ARCH-EVENTS.
5. **Switch off before Remove** (M9-Q4, recommendation). *Switch off* (with *Manage people*) ends a person's sign-in at once: sessions, access keys, OAuth grants of AI apps and the person's agents stop (M7-F36), invitations they made are taken back, and nothing else changes; *Switch on* gives the sign-in back. A switched-off person counts as nobody for notifications, picks and limits, and keeps every record. Enforced by: SEC-MATRIX (a switched-off person's session, key and agent are refused), ARCH-PERMS.
6. ***Remove a person* is final and has one plan.** With the new permission *Remove people* (organization, *Manage*; *Administrator* at the start; no key or agent), after a window that says what is deleted now, what stays with *Former user K7Q2* and what is kept by law until when, all read from the walk and the keep rules (ADR-0045). One change then:
   - deletes the account and everything that only serves it: password, passkeys, two-step data, sessions, access keys, grants, handle, settings and the choice of theme (M8-F74), private saved searches (M5-F62), drafts, locks and notes (M3-F34), notifications and follows (M4-F64), work times through `delete_work_times_in` (M8-F30), agents (M7-F36);
   - makes the person a tombstone: the name and e-mail are gone, a random code is drawn (`Former user K7Q2`, four signs from the alphabet of random issue IDs, unique, PRIV-13), and every reference shows *Former user …* through `PersonRef`;
   - scrubs the copies of personal values in the events: the before and after of the person's own settings (M2-F75), the name and text of their private searches (M5-F63), and the values that the deleted rows carried;
   - leaves the work days, their breaks and reasons, with the person's name for holders of *See working hours of others* only, until an administrator deletes them after the keep period (ADR-0035, decision 7), and the security log, whose entries name the person by ID and so read *Former user …* from then on.

   What cannot be done inside one change (the full-text index of the texts that change, with SQLite's `secure_delete` on the segments, M5-F52) is a job, `privacy.finish-removal`, queued in that change and safe to run again. Enforced by: ARCH-PERMS, SEC-MATRIX, ARCH-PERSON-REF, ARCH-EVENTS, ARCH-DB-PARITY (the removal tests on both databases).
7. **Texts the person wrote** (M9-Q3, recommendation): the window offers *Keep their comments, shown as Former user* (the start, an organization setting `privacy.removed-texts`) or *Delete their comments*. Issue descriptions, fields, time entries and files they made are business records of the organization and stay, shown as *Former user*; deleting them is the ordinary deletion of each. Enforced by: SEC-MATRIX, review (no other text is deleted by the choice).
8. **A removal survives a restore.** Each removal writes one line to a list outside the database (`privacy/removed.list` in the data folder: the person's ID and the moment, nothing else). After a restore from a backup older than a line, the restore runs the removal again for that ID before the server opens (ADR-0046). Enforced by: review (the restore test of ADR-0046 covers it).
9. **Corrections** (PRIV-06): a person corrects their own name; a change of their e-mail address takes effect when they open a link sent to the new address, and the old address is told. An administrator with *Manage people* corrects the name and e-mail address of others with a reason; the person is told, and the security log records it. Other personal data already has its own way to be corrected (work days with a reason, ADR-0035; work times, ADR-0030). Enforced by: SEC-MATRIX, ARCH-EVENTS.

## Consequences

- A new entity that names a person has to declare its links, or the build fails, so export and removal cannot forget it. Enforced by: DATA-MARKS.
- The tombstone keeps every reference valid: history, comments and issues still read, with *Former user …* where a name was.
- Removal is not undone. The window says so, and the plan is shown before anything happens.
- Backups older than a removal still hold the person until their keep period ends; the keep rule *Backups* (ADR-0045) bounds that time, and the removal list stops a restore from bringing the person back.

## Alternatives considered

- **Each feature writes its own export and removal code:** the exact failure ADR-0015 was written to avoid. Rejected.
- **Overwrite the person's ID in every row:** breaks the security log's chain and every foreign key, and gains nothing over a tombstone whose name and e-mail are gone. Rejected.
- **Delete everything the person wrote:** destroys the organization's records (issues, decisions, time booked for customers) that it may keep under Art. 17(3). Rejected; the choice covers comments only.
- **Export synchronously in the request:** a person with years of work produces a large file; a job keeps the server responsive. Rejected.

## Enforced by

DATA-MARKS, ARCH-PERMS, SEC-MATRIX, ARCH-PERSON-REF, ARCH-EVENTS, ARCH-DB-PARITY, DATA-LOGS; review (the one walk, and what a removal keeps).
