# EN 301 549 report

Where Bugaboo stands against the European standard for accessible ICT, EN 301 549 V3.2.1 (2021-03), clause by
clause, and against the criteria that WCAG 2.2 added at level AA after it (COMP-15, UX-04). It is the base of the
[model accessibility statement](../../userDocs/accessibility-statement.md) that an organization publishes.

**Version:** the browser app and the program of Bugaboo 0.6.0. **Date of the report:** 2026-11-21.

**Not tested by a person with a screen reader.** The screens passed the automatic checks (axe, the walk of the
keyboard, contrast, and the browser tests named below), but no person has yet used them with a screen reader. It was
decided on 2026-11-21 that the first release goes without such a pass. The criteria that only a screen reader can
tell are marked *Not tested by a person*, not *Supports*; the [checklist](screen-reader-checklist.md) is the way to
test them later.

## How it was checked

Bugaboo is a web application: its screens are web content in a browser, so clause 9 (web) applies to them, with the
WCAG 2.1 criteria at levels A and AA that it names, and the six criteria that WCAG 2.2 added at those levels. The
program `bugaboo` on the command line is software in the sense of clause 11: it writes plain text to the terminal of
the system and reads the keyboard. Clauses 6, 7, 8, 10 and 13 (voice, video, hardware, documents that are not web
pages, relay services) do not apply to a program that has none of these.

Each row gives one of five results:

- **Supports:** the criterion is met on every screen; the evidence names the test that shows it, or says why it is
  met by the way Bugaboo is built.
- **Partly supports:** met in part; the row says what is missing and the follow-up that will close it.
- **Does not support:** not met; the row says why.
- **Not applicable:** Bugaboo has nothing the criterion is about; the row says why.
- **Not tested by a person:** the automatic checks named in the row pass, but whether the criterion is met can only
  be told by a person with a screen reader, and nobody has tested it yet.

The machine measures run in the browser tests of TEST-A11Y, on every screen of the route table and every screen
before sign-in, on a computer and on a phone, on the sample organization as its administrator sees it:

- **axe** with the rules of WCAG 2.2 A and AA, in each of the four built-in themes and again with the device asking
  for more contrast (`a11y.spec.ts`, `a11y-privacy.spec.ts` and the other `a11y-*.spec.ts`; the board, the calendar,
  the relation map, dashboards, *Releases*, *Pinned* and the window *E-mail me this search* each have their own:
  `a11y-board-*.spec.ts`, `a11y-calendar-*.spec.ts`, `a11y-relation-map-computer.spec.ts`, `a11y-dashboards.spec.ts`,
  `a11y-releases.spec.ts` and `a11y-pins-and-mail.spec.ts`).
- **Reflow:** at 320 CSS pixels wide nothing scrolls sideways except the content that needs two dimensions, which
  WCAG 1.4.10 names as an exception: the issue lists (tables of columns), the matrix of access (a table) and the
  timeline (a chart of days) (`comp_15 every address reflows at 320 px without sideways scroll`).
- **Zoom and text spacing:** at the room of 200 % zoom no text is cut off and nothing scrolls sideways, and with the
  text spacing of WCAG 1.4.12 no text is cut off and no item draws over its neighbour
  (`comp_15 every address keeps its text at 200 % zoom and with wide text spacing`).
- **Targets:** every control is at least 24 pixels with a mouse and 44 with touch
  (`comp_15 every target is at least 24 px`, `ux_02_every_control_has_a_touch_target_of_at_least_44_px_on_a_phone`).
- **The keyboard:** every screen is walked with Tab from its start to its end: each stop shows its focus, no stop is
  covered by a part that stays in place, each stop comes after the one before it in the order of the code, no stop
  changes the address or opens a window of the browser, and the focus leaves the page at its end
  (`comp_15 a Tab walk of every screen keeps the order of the code, shows the focus and has no trap`). The walk reaches every control; it does not use them. That the main task of a screen can be done with the
  keyboard alone is proven only on the screens that have a test of their own for it: `issue-list-keyboard.spec.ts` (the issue list), `time-sheet-computer.spec.ts` (the timesheet), `time-quick-computer.spec.ts` (the timer), `a11y-timeline-computer.spec.ts`, `a11y-timeline-phone.spec.ts` and `a11y-timeline-people-computer.spec.ts` (the timeline, its card and its plan warnings), `a11y-privacy.spec.ts` (*Data exports* and the window *Remove a person*), `a11y-work-times.spec.ts` (the window *Add away time*), `a11y-time-factor.spec.ts` (the window *How long will it take you*), `theme-editor.spec.ts` (the colours of the theme editor), `assistant.spec.ts` (the assistant), `my-agents.spec.ts` (the rules of *My agents*) and `layouts.spec.ts` (the order of the fields of a type). The other screens have no
  such test yet (M9-F134).
- **Forced colours and motion:** under forced colours the focus, the rows and the states keep their outlines
  (`forced-colors.spec.ts`), the end of every box that scrolls draws a line where its shadow would be
  (`ux_7_2 under forced colors the ends of a box that scrolls draw a line where their shadow would be`), and *Less motion*, or the device asking for it, sets every motion to none
  (`thm_06 Less motion turns motion off, and so does the setting of the device`).

The four built-in themes set colours only (ADR-0026), so the measures of size and place are taken in one of them;
colours are measured by axe in all four.

What a machine cannot tell was checked by reading the screens and the code: names and roles of the timeline and the
windows, the words of errors, the status messages, the help in the same place, redundant entry and sign-in without a
test of memory. What only a screen reader can tell (whether names, the order of reading and announcements make sense
when heard) has not been tested by a person: those rows say *Not tested by a person*.

## What the measures found and what was fixed

The first run of the measures on every screen found these, and the pictures of the pass two more; each was fixed in
the part it was about:

| Criterion | Where | What was wrong | Fixed |
|-----------|-------|----------------|-------|
| 1.4.10 Reflow | *Timesheet* on a window of 320 px | The seven day tabs did not fit and the page scrolled sideways | The days take a second row when seven fingers do not fit, in the same columns as the row above |
| 2.1.1 Keyboard | Every window | A body that scrolled with nothing in it that takes the focus could not be scrolled with the keyboard (axe, seen once under load) | The body takes the focus itself in that case |
| 2.4.3 Focus order | Every setting that is typed in | Enter saved the value and the box was made anew, so the focus fell to the page | The box stays and takes the new value; the focus stays in it |
| 2.4.7 Focus visible | The place for files on an issue | Its button for a drop with the keyboard showed no focus | The ring is drawn around the whole place |
| 2.4.11 Focus not obscured | The theme editor | A group at the foot of the box went under the bar of *Save theme* | What the keyboard brings into view stops above the bar |
| 2.4.11 Focus not obscured | *About*, *Show more parts* | The list above read its next pages by itself and moved as it drew its rows, which pushed the focused button out of sight | The list reads more only with *Show more parts*; *Show more* brings itself back into view for a few frames after the keyboard brought the focus to it or pressed it, never for a mouse, and stops as soon as the person scrolls |
| UX §1.1 (padding) | *Agent keys* on a phone | The words of a key took the height of a list, an empty area above *Renew* (M9-F88) | The words are as tall as their lines |
| UX §1.1 (padding) | *Keep and delete rules* | Under a period that was not changed the line of the note stood empty (M9-F93) | The line says how to see what another period would delete |
| 4.1.3 Status messages | *My data*, the file | Being created and ready changed without a word to a screen reader | The part of the file is a status |

Targets under 24 px were found only on the bars of the timeline, which are alone in their rows with the room of 24 px
around them, the spacing that WCAG 2.5.8 allows; the measure takes that room into account.
